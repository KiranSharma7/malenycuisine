Maleny Cuisine design system

Everything below is extracted from index.html, product.html and styles.css — nothing here is invented. Build every new page from these tokens and components and the site will hold together. Start from page-template.html, which already carries the promotion, header, mega menu, mobile drawer and footer.

The one rule above all the others: if a value already has a token, use the token. A raw hex, a raw shadow or a fresh rgba() in a new page is how a design system dies.

Colour

One warm brown family carried from near-black to pale linen, plus the grounds it sits on. There is no second hue anywhere in the system. Emphasis is made with tone and weight, never with colour.

Browns

  • --chocolate #4a261c The structural brown. Announcement bar, the ground behind the footer photograph, and the tint under an icon hover.
  • --chocolate-deep #32150f The closer. Headings, button borders and labels, filled buttons, and every dark band below the hero.
  • --ink #2d150f Body and interface text on pale grounds. Redder and softer than the deep brown, so long reading never feels stamped on.
  • --night #171310 The hero's ground and the fallback behind hero photography. Appears once per page and never below the fold.

Grounds

  • --paper #ffffff The default ground of every content band below the hero, and of the product card.
  • --ivory #f8f4ec The chrome ground: header, search panel, mega menu, mobile drawer and footer. Also the separator ground when two paper bands would otherwise touch.
  • --cream #eee4d6 One step down from ivory. Mega-menu cards, drawer thumbs, quiet fills — how a surface separates without a shadow.
  • --lamplight #f7ede1 Type and strokes on dark grounds only. Ivory warmed and lifted so it survives against photography.

Ink at reduced strength

Three steps, and nothing between them. Reach for these instead of writing a fresh rgba() — a fourth opacity is how the palette starts to blur.

TokenValueUse for
--ink-muted rgba(45, 21, 15, 0.72) Supporting copy under a heading; an eyebrow; a credit line.
--ink-soft rgba(45, 21, 15, 0.65) Sub-labels, placeholders, the resting label of an unselected chip.
--ink-quiet rgba(45, 21, 15, 0.55) The quietest a control may go while still being readable.

Never use a reduced-strength ink for dietary, allergen or ingredient text. That copy is regulated and always runs at full --ink on a solid pale ground.

Rules and edges

TokenValueUse for
--hairline rgba(74, 38, 28, 0.2) The default divider: band separators, footer rules, card media edge.
--hairline-soft rgba(74, 38, 28, 0.12) An edge that should barely register — a resting chip, a drawer card.
--hairline-strong rgba(74, 38, 28, 0.45) A card edge answering to hover or focus.
--rule-on-dark rgba(247, 237, 225, 0.16) Dividers inside a dark band.

Typography

A high-contrast Caslon does all the speaking; DM Sans does every job that has to be read quickly and repeatedly. The pairing is deliberately lopsided — the serif is rare and large, the sans is everywhere and quiet.

The three faces

--font-script "Mrs Saint Delafield", …, cursive The founders' signed name, once on the site. A drawn mark, not a third text face.

The tracking ladder

Uppercase text is always tracked, and the smaller it is the wider it tracks. Uppercase at default tracking is off-system.

Nothing on this site is set smaller than 12px. If a label will not fit at 12px, the layout is wrong — do not go to 11px.

Space & layout

The page is full-bleed, not boxed. One fluid gutter runs down both edges and every band shares it. There is no page-wide max-width container; measure caps do the work instead.

Tokens

--gutter clamp(28px, 3.2vw, 64px) Horizontal padding on the header and on every full-width band. Never hand-pick another.
--section-y clamp(64px, 7.5vw, 118px) A content band's top padding.
--section-y-end clamp(56px, 6vw, 96px) Its bottom padding.
--head-gap clamp(36px, 4vw, 64px) The gap from a band's heading down to its content.

Inside a component the rhythm is 4 / 8 / 10 / 14 / 16 / 24 / 32. Between bands it is always a fluid clamp, never a fixed number.

Shape & edges

Corners are almost square, on a three-value scale with nothing between. Pill-shaped buttons and chips are not part of this system — the pill radius is for circles only.

4px

--radius-sm2pxButtons, inputs, chips, the newsletter field.
--radius-md4pxCards, image tiles, panels, the promotion dialog.

Borders are hairlines. 1px solid --chocolate-deep for a control outline; 1px of a --hairline* token for structure. There are no thick strokes, no double rules and no decorative frames.

Depth

The system is flat. Depth comes from tonal layering — paper, ivory, cream, deep brown — and from hairlines, not from shadow. Only three shadows exist, and each one is attached to a state or to a job, never to a resting surface.

--shadow-lift Hover on a card or tile, paired with a 3px rise.
--shadow-panel A panel overlaying the page — the open mega menu.
--shadow-hero-text Legibility insurance for display type over photography. Not a style choice; never reuse off the hero.

Flat-at-rest. No surface carries a shadow in its default state. If a card needs to look separate while idle, change its tone to cream or give it a hairline — do not add a shadow.

Motion

Short and ease-only. Nothing bounces, nothing springs, nothing slides in from off-screen except the mobile drawer. Motion is a response to the visitor, not decoration.

TokenValueUse for
--dur-fast160msA small state flip: a tab, a drawer card edge.
--dur200msThe default: colour, background, transform on a control.
--dur-slow220msA card lift, an underline wipe, a panel opening.
--dur-image400msImage scale on hover. Reserved — nothing else takes 400ms.
--dur-reveal520msThe once-only settle-upward as a band enters view.

The site's signature: on hover a card rises at 220ms while its image scales at 400ms. Two speeds, one gesture. Use .lift and .lift__media to get it.

Band reveal

Content settles upward once, the first time its band comes into view. The band is only armed by script when the browser can observe it and the visitor accepts motion — so copy is never left invisible by a failed script. 

<section class="section" id="story">
  <h2 class="section-title" data-reveal>Our story</h2>
  <p class="lede" data-reveal data-reveal-delay="1">…</p>
  <a class="button" href="#shop" data-reveal data-reveal-delay="2">Shop the range</a>
</section>

<!-- then in script.js: -->
armBandReveal(document.querySelector("#story"));

prefers-reduced-motion is honoured globally: every transition collapses to 0.01ms, smooth scrolling turns off, and the recipes marquee becomes an ordinary scrollable row. Anything you add must survive that.

Imagery

Photography carries all the warmth in this system, which is why the chrome around it stays so plain.

KindTreatment
Packshot Shot on white. object-fit: contain with mix-blend-mode: multiply so the white drops onto the card ground. Square frame, 640×640.
Lifestyle object-fit: cover, mix-blend-mode: normal. Add .product-card__media--photo on a card whose main image is lifestyle, not a packshot.
Category tile Framed at the photograph's own ratio via --tile-ratio, not forced into one crop. Wide group shots 2.36:1, standard tiles 1.62:1.
Dish / recipe Square, 1:1, no mat and no hairline — the picture sits straight on the ground.
Band background A real <img> behind the copy at z-index: -2, plus a scrim ::before at -1 once the band narrows. Text is always real HTML, never baked into the picture.

Every <img> below the fold carries width, height, loading="lazy" and decoding="async". A decorative image takes alt=""; a meaningful one describes what is in the frame, not what the product is called.

The named rules

These are the decisions that make the site look like one site. Breaking one is allowed, but only on purpose and only once you have said so out loud.

  • The One Family Rule There is no second accent hue. Every colour is the same warm brown at a different lightness, plus the ground it sits on. If a screen needs emphasis, change tone or weight — never introduce a colour. This is why the sale price uses a dimmed strike-through rather than red.
  • The Doorway Rule --night appears once per page, in the hero, and never below it. The dark is an entrance, not the house — the page turns to daylight from the fold down.
  • The Dark Band Allowance A page may carry one dark band below the hero, in --chocolate-deep with lamplight type — never --night, so the Doorway Rule still holds. The home page spends its allowance twice, on the ethos band and the pantry CTA, because the CTA is photography with a brown fallback rather than a flat brown band. A new page gets one. Do not spend it on a band that could sit on paper.
  • The Rare Serif Rule Libre Caslon Display appears at most twice per screen — practically, that is the band's heading and, if the band has one, its statement. A voice that speaks constantly stops being heard. This is why product names in cards are set in DM Sans.
  • The Tracking Ladder Rule Uppercase is always tracked: 15px → 0.12em, 14px → 0.11em, 12px → 0.08em. Uppercase at default tracking is off-system.
  • The Flat-At-Rest Rule No surface carries a shadow in its default state. Shadow is a response to hover or focus, or the mark of a panel floating over the page. Nothing else.
  • The Dietary Contrast Rule Dietary, allergen and ingredient text is safety information. It is set at full --ink on --paper, --ivory or --cream — never over photography, never in a translucent panel, never at reduced opacity, never clipped by a line clamp. The one dark-ground exception is the ethos band's marketing copy, set in full-strength lamplight on solid brown (≈13:1). Actual label text stays pale-ground.
  • The Evidence Rule There is no visual vocabulary for star ratings, review counts, testimonials, awards, stockist addresses or delivery estimates — because there is no evidence for them. Do not invent the component; ask the client for the content first.
  • The Mutual Exclusion Rule The mega menu, the search panel and the mobile drawer are never open at once. Opening any one closes the other two, and Escape closes all three.

Links

The site has one link signature: a 1px underline that wipes in over 220ms. The direction is the vocabulary — the header wipes in from the right, content links wipe in from the left, and a centred label opens from its middle. Never a colour change.

From the right — header nav From the left — content & footer From the centre — a centred tile

A placeholder is never the only label. Every field gets a real <label>, visually hidden if the design has no room for it.

Labelled field — the one to build a form from

.field · the 58px box again, with the label sitting inside the top of it rather than floating away on interaction: someone correcting an address or an enquiry is reading the label and the value at the same time, and the label they need most is the one that disappears once the field is filled. It lives in styles.css and is shared by the checkout, Work With Us and Contact Us — a second copy is how two forms start to drift apart.

Three states anyone can tell apart without reading: resting is a hairline on paper, focus is a full-strength edge plus the ring, and invalid drops the ground to cream. The invalid state changes tone rather than hue, which is what the One Family Rule asks for — and the message underneath carries the meaning, so nothing here depends on colour alone. Every required field is silent about it and every optional one says so, because the reverse marks a whole form with asterisks to flag the exception.

That does not look like an email address. Check for a typo.

Search — the written line

.search-field · no box. A transparent field on a single deep-brown bottom rule, set in Caslon at 25px, so it reads as a written line rather than a form control. Reserved for the header search panel.

Drawer search — the filled field

Dark hero — the doorway

Fact list

.fact-list · short lines of fact divided by hairlines, and .fact-list--split for two columns above 720px — never more than two, because four columns of one short line each stops reading as a list. This is what a band of plain facts uses instead of a grid of same-size cards: a card each would spend more attention on the box than on the line inside it. Regulated dietary text is allowed here, at full ink on paper or ivory, never clipped.

Indexed answers — the FAQ

.faq-layout · a sticky .faq-index beside a column of open .faq-item answers, capped at 1180px and collapsing to one column at 900px. Deliberately not an accordion. Two of the FAQ's answers are dietary claims, and the Dietary Contrast Rule will not have regulated text sitting behind a click; once those two have to be open, all of them do, or the page teaches the reader that some answers are hidden. Questions are set in DM Sans, not Caslon — six serif headings on one screen would break the Rare Serif Rule six times over.

Closing band

.page-cta · one Caslon line and one button on ivory, as the last thing before the footer. It is the quiet alternative to the full-bleed CTA over photography, for a page whose dark allowance is already spent or which never had a photograph.

Rail band

Heading, a filter row and arrows, then a horizontally scrolling rail with mandatory snap. Four cards fill the rail at full width, stepping to 3 / 2 / one-and-a-bit as the viewport narrows.

The rail's structural trick: contain: paint on the scroller. Without it the off-screen cards still counted towards the document's scrollable width — the root reported 4759px on a 414px phone — and iOS Safari let the whole page be dragged sideways, ignoring overflow-x: hidden on the root. Any new horizontal scroller you add needs contain: paint too.

Dark statement band

.section--deep · proof marks separated by hairlines rather than boxes, then a statement and a copy column. Below 780px the marks stack into full-width rows and the hairlines pull out through the gutter so they run edge to edge.

Full-bleed CTA over photography

.pantry-cta · a real <img> at z-index: -2, a scrim ::before that only appears below 900px, and the copy sitting above optical centre because the product row occupies the lower half of the photograph. All words stay selectable HTML.

Assurance bar

.assurance · the footer's top rail, and the announcement bar's twin: the page opens on a Chocolate bar and closes on the same one. Six claims drift as one endless rail, separated by --rule-on-dark hairlines. The bar counts as chrome rather than a content band, so it spends none of the Dark Band Allowance.

One custom property, --assurance-gap, drives the space between claims, the left padding that centres each hairline in that space, and the keyframe's seam correction. The six claims are repeated three times so the rail never runs short on a wide monitor; copies two and three carry aria-hidden="true" and live in the markup rather than in script, so the loop still closes with JavaScript off. Keep the copy count in the markup and the copies option in armMarquee in step, or the loop seam will drift. Script only holds the speed at 60px per second and runs the pause button.

Claims are kept to three words or fewer, apart from the shipping threshold, which has to stay precise. That is what lets all six sit on screen together from about 1200px up.

Dietary, allergen and ingredient text is not allowed on this bar. Regulated claims have to stand still at full ink on a pale ground, under the Dietary Contrast Rule.

Overlays

Promotion dialog

A real modal: role="dialog", aria-modal="true", labelled and described, focus moved to the close button on open and returned to the previous element on close, Tab trapped inside, Escape and backdrop click both close it, and the body scroll locked while it is up. It appears 550ms after load.

It is a 2.13:1 landscape dialog on desktop and a 0.66 portrait sheet below 1000px, with a separate art file for each and a third layout for short screens (max-height: 680px). If you change the copy, check all three.

Fixed top-left, translated out of view until focused. Every page needs it as its first element, pointing at the id on <main>.

Visually hidden

.visually-hidden · for text that a screen reader needs and the design does not show — a field label, a "Was"/"now" price qualifier, a live region.

Icons

One icon language: a 24×24 viewBox, no fill, stroke: currentColor, round caps and joins, and a 1.5–1.6 stroke weight. They are drawn inline so they inherit colour from their button.

Two exceptions, both deliberate: the rail arrows use stroke-linecap: square and linejoin: miter so they read as chevrons rather than as soft strokes; the ethos proof marks and the social icons are filled shapes, not strokes.

Page recipes

How to compose the pages still to be built, using only what is already here. Every one starts from page-template.html.

Category page (Chutneys, Sauces, …)

Dark hero with the category name → paper band with the section heading and a grid of product cards (not a rail — there is no "more" to scroll to) → a paper band of dish tiles for that category → the pantry CTA → footer.

Product page

Use the Serve First recipe. The serving photograph is the entrance: a 60/40 split with one full-bleed product-in-scene image left, then breadcrumb, name, price, short pairing line, verified dietary claims, quantity and a full-width Add to cart action on Ivory. This is the one page where the Doorway Rule is spent differently: the meal and packshot replace the dark hero. Do not add both. The jar and meal must be flattened into one production image rather than layered at runtime. Open the implemented reference page.

Our Story / About

Dark hero → .prose in a paper band → the dark statement band with the proof marks → the two-column family feature → footer. This page is Read mode: structure for comprehension first, and let the serif appear only twice per screen.

Contact / FAQ / policies

No hero. Open straight onto a paper band with the section heading and .prose at --measure. A form uses the newsletter field treatment. Keep the page short and let the footer carry the address and email — they are already there.

Stockists / Food Service

Dark hero → a paper band of copy → whatever the content actually is. Do not build a map component, a stockist card or a wholesale pricing table until the client supplies the data — see the Evidence Rule.

Accessibility floor

The home page already ships all of this. It is the floor, not the target — a new page that drops any of it is a regression.

Every page carries

  • A skip link as the first element in <body>.
  • One <h1>, and headings that descend without skipping a level.
  • aria-labelledby on every <section>, pointing at its own heading.
  • aria-label on every <nav> — "Main navigation", "Shop", "Help", "Legal", "Social media".
  • An aria-label on every icon-only control, and aria-hidden="true" on its SVG.
  • A real <label> for every input, visually hidden if need be.
  • A visible focus state on everything focusable.
  • A polite live region for anything that changes without a page load.
  • Decorative images at alt=""; clones out of the tab order with tabindex="-1" and aria-hidden.
  • A real pause control for anything that moves for more than five seconds (WCAG 2.2.2).

Known gaps to fix, not to copy

  • The header search input has no visible focus treatment of its own.
  • There is no styled aria-current state for the nav.
  • The mobile drawer does not trap focus the way the promotion dialog does.
  • The promotion appears on every page load with no memory of being dismissed.

Ship checklist

Run this before calling a new page done.

Do

  • Ground every screen in --paper or --ivory with --ink text.
  • Use a token wherever one exists — colour, shadow, duration, radius, gutter.
  • Keep to the three-radius scale: 2px controls, 4px surfaces, 999px circles only.
  • Track every uppercase string on the ladder.
  • Set dietary and allergen text at full ink on a solid pale ground.
  • Pair :hover with :focus-visible on every interactive element.
  • Give every below-fold image width, height, loading="lazy" and decoding="async".
  • Add contain: paint to any new horizontal scroller.
  • Check the page at 1440, 1180, 900, 860, 620 and 375px.
  • Check it once with reduced motion on.

Don't

  • Introduce a second accent hue, or a fourth ink opacity.
  • Put a resting shadow on any surface.
  • Use --night below the hero, or run two flat dark bands on one page.
  • Set Libre Caslon more than twice per screen, or use it for body copy.
  • Use a pill-shaped button, chip or input.
  • Let the announcement bar grow past 34px — truncate instead.
  • Let the mega menu, search panel and drawer be open at once.
  • Go below 12px, or use uppercase without tracking.
  • Invent ratings, reviews, awards, stockists or shipping promises.
  • Paraphrase a regulated dietary claim, or drop the Special BBQ Sauce carve-out from a range-wide statement.
